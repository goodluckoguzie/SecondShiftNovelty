Second Shift

Turns two weeks of exhaustion into one page of evidence.

What it is

A voice-first AI agent for unpaid family carers of people with dementia. Speak naturally at home. It logs care events, spots missed-medicine and symptom patterns, and auto-generates an evidence-cited GP / memory-clinic brief.

The user

Ravi, 34, works night shifts and cares for his 71-year-old father, who has dementia and takes 6 medicines. He is untrained, exhausted, and the only person who knows what happened at home this week. At the GP appointment, he forgets half of it.

Why it matters

  • ~982,000 people with dementia in the UK
  • Unpaid care is 50% of dementia’s £42bn cost
  • A third of dementia carers give 100+ hours a week
  • Home observations rarely reach the GP

How it works

Voice→ Log→ Patterns→ GP brief

The carer speaks → speech-to-text → AI extracts structured events → a rules engine flags patterns → one tap produces a cited PDF for the GP or memory clinic.

What’s novel

No UK product found that connects what the dementia carer observes at home to what the GP reads before the appointment.

Product Covers Missing vs. Second Shift
Jointly (Carers UK) Circle of care, tasks, medicine lists Tap-based; no voice logging, patterns, or GP brief
Share2Care (NHS WY) Carer app, meds, contingency plan in NHS record Organisation tool, not voice → patterns → GP brief
Heidi / Tortus / Accurx AI scribe of the GP appointment Knows nothing about what happened at home
Curendi (NHS CEP) Dementia carer guidance between appointments Advice and diaries, not voice → patterns → GP brief
Four novelty points
  • Home → GP closed loop
  • Voice-first for the carer, not the patient
  • Evidence-cited briefs (every line traces to a voice log)
  • Family handover briefs (“your sister takes over tomorrow”)
Safety stance

Organises notes and drafts questions for the GP. Never diagnoses, triages, or gives treatment advice. Not a medical device.

The 30-second demo

11pm. Ravi says: “Gave dad his 8pm meds, 40 minutes late. More confused again this evening, third time this week.” The agent logs it, flags the pattern after Tuesday’s dose change, and generates a one-page brief with timestamps, symptoms, and 5 questions for the GP.

Ravi walks into the appointment with evidence instead of exhaustion.
